Partial correlations with the seascorr functions

Jernej Jevšenak

2026-11-23

1 Introduction

Temperature and precipitation are correlated: in many regions, warm months are also dry. A simple correlation between a tree-ring chronology and temperature therefore partly reflects precipitation, and the other way round. A partial correlation removes the linear effect of a second, control variable from both the chronology and the variable of interest, and describes the relationship that remains.

monthly_response_seascorr() and daily_response_seascorr() calculate partial correlations for all moving windows of monthly or daily climate data (Jevšenak 2020), just as monthly_response() and daily_response() calculate simple correlations. For every window, the primary variable (env_data_primary) and the control variable (env_data_control) are aggregated over the same months or days, and the partial correlation between the response and the primary variable, controlling for the control variable, is calculated.

The functions build on seascorr (Meko et al. 2011), which summarises the relationship with a primary climate variable by simple correlations and the relationship with a secondary variable by partial correlations, controlling for the primary variable. In dendroTools, the partial correlations are calculated for env_data_primary, which therefore corresponds to the secondary variable of seascorr. The simple correlations of the other variable can be calculated with monthly_response() or daily_response(). With pcor_control = TRUE, its partial correlations, controlling for env_data_primary, are calculated as well, so that both variables are described in the same way.

2 Data and main arguments

The response is a data frame with one column and years as row names. Climate data can be given in wide format, with years as row names and 12 months or 366 days as columns, or in tidy format, with the columns Year, Month (or DOY) and the climate variable. The two climate variables are aggregated separately, typically by means for temperature and by sums for precipitation.

Argument Purpose
env_data_primary, env_data_control climate data; partial correlations are calculated for the primary variable
tidy_env_data_primary, tidy_env_data_control TRUE for climate data in tidy format
aggregate_function_env_data_primary, aggregate_function_env_data_control "mean", "median", "sum", "quantile" or "SPEI", and for daily data also "min" and "max"
fixed_width, or lower_limit and upper_limit window widths in months or days
previous_year, number_previous_years, month_interval or day_interval windows that extend into previous years
pcor_method, pcor_na_use Pearson, Spearman or Kendall partial correlations, and the treatment of missing values
remove_insignificant, remove_insignificant_method, alpha significance filter
boot, boot_n, boot_ci_type bootstrap confidence intervals
pcor_control partial correlations for the control variable as well
dc_method and the other dc_* arguments climate detrending
skip_window_length, skip_window_position faster daily analyses, by skipping window widths and positions

The results are summarised by summary(). plot(x, type = 1) shows the window width with the strongest partial correlation, plot(x, type = 2) the heatmap of all partial correlations and, with pcor_control = TRUE, plot(x, type = 3) the heatmap of the partial correlations of the control variable. The aggregated climate of the optimal window is in $optimized_return, and $variance_partitioning describes how the two variables share the explained variance in that window (section 3.2).

3 Monthly data

3.1 Temperature, controlling for precipitation

The mean vessel area chronology (data_MVA) is related to the monthly mean temperatures of Ljubljana, controlling for the monthly precipitation sums. The temperatures are in wide format and the precipitation in tidy format.

library(dendroTools)

data(data_MVA)
data(LJ_monthly_temperatures)
data(LJ_monthly_precipitation)

monthly_pcor <- monthly_response_seascorr(
  response = data_MVA,
  env_data_primary = LJ_monthly_temperatures,
  env_data_control = LJ_monthly_precipitation,
  tidy_env_data_primary = FALSE,
  tidy_env_data_control = TRUE,
  aggregate_function_env_data_primary = "mean",
  aggregate_function_env_data_control = "sum",
  row_names_subset = TRUE,
  remove_insignificant = FALSE)

summary(monthly_pcor)

3.2 Both variables

With pcor_control = TRUE, the function also calculates, for every window, the partial correlation of MVA with precipitation, controlling for temperature. These are exactly the partial correlations that a second run with the two variables, and their settings, swapped would return. The partial correlations of temperature do not change.

monthly_both <- monthly_response_seascorr(
  response = data_MVA,
  env_data_primary = LJ_monthly_temperatures,
  env_data_control = LJ_monthly_precipitation,
  tidy_env_data_primary = FALSE,
  tidy_env_data_control = TRUE,
  aggregate_function_env_data_control = "sum",
  row_names_subset = TRUE,
  remove_insignificant = FALSE,
  pcor_control = TRUE)

# the main rows of summary()
key_results <- function(x) {
  s <- summary(x)
  s[s$Variable %in% c("maximal_calculated_metric",
                      "optimal_time_window",
                      "maximal_calculated_metric_control",
                      "optimal_time_window_control",
                      "control_metric_in_optimal_time_window"), ]
}

key_results(monthly_both)
#>                                 Variable     Value
#> 5              maximal_calculated_metric     0.681
#> 12                   optimal_time_window Mar - Nov
#> 14     maximal_calculated_metric_control     0.453
#> 17           optimal_time_window_control Jul - Sep
#> 19 control_metric_in_optimal_time_window     0.168

summary() now also gives the strongest partial correlation of the control variable and its window, and the partial correlation of the control variable in the optimal window of the primary variable. Figure 1 shows both surfaces.

Figure 1: Partial correlations between MVA and (a) monthly temperature, controlling for precipitation, and (b) monthly precipitation, controlling for temperature. Each panel has its own colour scale.

The two partial correlations do not divide the climate signal into a temperature part and a precipitation part: the variance of MVA that temperature and precipitation share, because the two variables are correlated, belongs to neither. $variance_partitioning describes this split for the optimal window. It gives the three simple correlations, both partial correlations, the variance of MVA explained by both variables together (r_squared), the parts unique to the primary and the control variable, and the shared part, which is negative when one variable suppresses the other.

In the optimal window, almost all of the explained variance is unique to temperature. Note that this window, from March to November, is long, because MVA and the temperatures share a positive trend; section 5 shows how detrending changes this.

3.3 Significance

The significance filter is applied to both surfaces with the same method and settings. Methods that correct for multiple testing ("fdr_BY", "fdr_BH", "sidak", "maxstat" and "cluster") correct within each surface, as a separate run with the two variables swapped would, and the permutation methods use the same permutations for both surfaces. The filters are described in $significance_filter and $significance_filter_control.

monthly_fdr <- monthly_response_seascorr(
  response = data_MVA,
  env_data_primary = LJ_monthly_temperatures,
  env_data_control = LJ_monthly_precipitation,
  tidy_env_data_primary = FALSE,
  tidy_env_data_control = TRUE,
  aggregate_function_env_data_control = "sum",
  row_names_subset = TRUE,
  remove_insignificant = TRUE,
  remove_insignificant_method = "fdr_BH",
  pcor_control = TRUE)

monthly_fdr$significance_filter$criterion
#> [1] "Adjusted p < 0.05 (BH)"

# windows retained by the filter, out of all windows
c(primary = monthly_fdr$significance_filter$retained_cells,
  control = monthly_fdr$significance_filter_control$retained_cells,
  windows = sum(!is.na(monthly_both$calculations)))
#> primary control windows 
#>      75       4      78

4 Daily data

daily_response_seascorr() works in the same way with daily data. Here every window is 45 days wide (fixed_width = 45), which keeps the example fast. Windows of different widths are evaluated with lower_limit and upper_limit, and skip_window_length and skip_window_position then reduce the computing time by evaluating only every n-th window width or position.

data(LJ_daily_temperatures)
data(LJ_daily_precipitation)

daily_both <- daily_response_seascorr(
  response = data_MVA,
  env_data_primary = LJ_daily_temperatures,
  env_data_control = LJ_daily_precipitation,
  tidy_env_data_primary = FALSE,
  tidy_env_data_control = TRUE,
  aggregate_function_env_data_control = "sum",
  row_names_subset = TRUE,
  fixed_width = 45,
  remove_insignificant = FALSE,
  pcor_control = TRUE)

With one window width, both sets of partial correlations can be shown as lines through the year (Figure 2). The dashed lines give the partial correlation that is significant at p = 0.05 for a single window; one degree of freedom is spent on the controlled variable.

n_years <- nrow(daily_both$optimized_return)
t_critical <- stats::qt(0.975, df = n_years - 3)
r_critical <- t_critical / sqrt(n_years - 3 + t_critical ^ 2)

n_windows <- ncol(daily_both$calculations)
labels <- c("temperature, controlling for precipitation",
            "precipitation, controlling for temperature")

daily_lines <- data.frame(
  start = rep(seq_len(n_windows), 2),
  partial_r = c(daily_both$calculations[1, ],
                daily_both$calculations_control[1, ]),
  variable = factor(rep(labels, each = n_windows), levels = labels)
)

ggplot(daily_lines, aes(x = start, y = partial_r, colour = variable)) +
  geom_hline(yintercept = 0, colour = "grey60") +
  geom_hline(yintercept = c(-r_critical, r_critical), colour = "grey60",
             linetype = "dashed") +
  geom_line(na.rm = TRUE) +
  scale_colour_manual(values = c("#2a78d6", "#eb6834")) +
  labs(x = "First day of the window (day of year)",
       y = "Partial correlation", colour = NULL) +
  theme_bw() +
  theme(legend.position = "bottom", panel.grid.minor = element_blank())

Figure 2: Partial correlations between MVA and 45-day windows of temperature, controlling for precipitation, and of precipitation, controlling for temperature. The dashed lines show the critical partial correlation at p = 0.05.

The strongest relationship of MVA is with spring temperature, from late March to early May. In this window, precipitation has almost no partial correlation, and nearly all of the explained variance is unique to temperature. Precipitation has its own, positive partial correlation with MVA in late summer. The partial correlations with temperature are positive in almost every window, partly because MVA and the temperatures share a trend (section 5).

5 Climate detrending

The aggregated climate of every window can be detrended before the partial correlations are calculated (see the vignette Detrending_climate_data). By default, both variables are detrended with the same settings. dc_env_data_control changes any of them for the control variable, for example ratios for precipitation sums while temperature, which can be negative, is detrended by differences.

monthly_detrended <- monthly_response_seascorr(
  response = data_MVA,
  env_data_primary = LJ_monthly_temperatures,
  env_data_control = LJ_monthly_precipitation,
  tidy_env_data_primary = FALSE,
  tidy_env_data_control = TRUE,
  aggregate_function_env_data_control = "sum",
  row_names_subset = TRUE,
  remove_insignificant = FALSE,
  dc_method = "Spline",
  dc_difference = TRUE,
  dc_env_data_control = list(dc_difference = FALSE),
  pcor_control = TRUE)

key_results(monthly_detrended)
#>                                 Variable     Value
#> 5              maximal_calculated_metric     0.352
#> 12                   optimal_time_window       Apr
#> 14     maximal_calculated_metric_control     0.242
#> 17           optimal_time_window_control Jul - Sep
#> 19 control_metric_in_optimal_time_window    -0.006

After detrending, the optimal temperature window is short and lies in spring, as in the daily analysis, instead of covering March to November.

6 Notes on interpretation

  • A partial correlation describes the relationship that remains after the linear effect of the other variable is removed. When the two variables are strongly correlated in a window, both partial correlations can be small even though together they explain much of the response; $variance_partitioning shows how large the shared part is.
  • pcor_control = TRUE gives the same partial correlations, intervals and significance filter as a separate run with the two variables, and their settings, swapped.
  • The variance split is defined for Pearson correlations. With pcor_method = "spearman", it refers to ranks, and with "kendall" it is not given.
  • Bootstrap confidence intervals of type "bca" need at least as many bootstrap replicates (boot_n) as analysed years.
